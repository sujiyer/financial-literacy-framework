# Module: Banking Basics

**For educators, financial counsellors, and member-facing staff**

This module is for people opening their first bank account, people returning to banking after a gap, and anyone who wants to understand how the banking system actually works rather than just how to use their debit card.

---

## Core Concepts

### Concept 1: What a Bank Account Actually Is

A bank account is an agreement between you and a financial institution. You deposit money. They hold it safely, protect it up to $250,000 through FDIC or NCUA insurance, and give you ways to access it. In return, they may pay you a small amount of interest on your balance, and they may charge fees for certain services.

**Checking account:** For everyday spending. You can access money freely via debit card, checks, or transfers. Usually pays little or no interest.

**Savings account:** For money you want to keep separate and grow slowly. Pays higher interest (APY) than checking. May limit how many withdrawals you can make per month.

**The most important thing to know:** Your money in an FDIC or NCUA insured account is safe up to $250,000 per account category, even if the bank or credit union fails. This protection is free and automatic.

---

### Concept 2: How Banks Make Money — And Why That Matters for You

Banks and credit unions are not keeping your money in a vault. They are lending most of it out to other customers as loans, mortgages, and business credit. They charge borrowers interest on those loans. That is how they make money.

This is important because it explains two things:
- Why banks want your deposits — more deposits means more money to lend
- Why the fees and interest rates on products matter — that is their revenue

**Credit unions are different:** Credit unions are member-owned cooperatives. Their goal is to serve members, not to generate profit. This often means lower fees, better interest rates on savings, and lower rates on loans. But they are usually smaller and may have fewer ATM locations.

---

### Concept 3: Understanding Fees

Bank fees can quietly drain your account if you are not watching for them. The most common ones:

| Fee Type | What Triggers It | How to Avoid It |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly maintenance fee | Just having the account | Meet minimum balance or use direct deposit |
| Overdraft fee | Spending more than your balance | Keep a buffer; opt out of overdraft coverage |
| ATM fee | Using an out-of-network ATM | Use your bank's ATMs or get cash back at stores |
| Returned payment fee | A payment bounces | Ensure funds are available before payment dates |

**Plain language key point:** Read the fee schedule when you open an account. Ask specifically: what fees can I be charged, and how do I avoid them?

---

### Concept 4: Direct Deposit and Why It Matters

Direct deposit means your employer or government benefit payment goes straight into your account electronically, rather than as a paper check.

Benefits of direct deposit:
- Money is available immediately — no waiting for a check to clear
- Many accounts waive monthly fees if you have direct deposit
- More secure than a paper check that can be lost or stolen
- Required for many higher-yield savings accounts

Setting up direct deposit requires giving your employer your bank's routing number (9 digits) and your account number (found on your bank statement or the bottom of a check).

## Glossary

**FDIC insurance** — Federal government protection for your deposits at banks, up to $250,000 per account category.

**NCUA insurance** — Federal government protection for deposits at credit unions, same $250,000 limit.

**APY (Annual Percentage Yield)** — The actual interest rate your savings earns in a year, accounting for compounding. A higher APY means your savings grow faster.

**Overdraft** — Spending more than your account balance. May result in a declined transaction or an overdraft fee, depending on your account settings.

**Routing number** — The 9-digit number identifying your bank, used for wire transfers, direct deposit setup, and electronic payments.

**Minimum balance** — The amount you must keep in your account to avoid a fee or to earn interest.

**Direct deposit** — Electronic transfer of payment directly into your bank account.

## For Educators — How to Use This Module

**Key message:** Banking is a tool. Understanding how it works puts you in control of it rather than the other way around.

**Common misconceptions to address:**
- "Banks are not safe" — FDIC and NCUA insurance is real and has never failed to protect an eligible deposit
- "I need a lot of money to open an account" — many accounts have no minimum opening deposit
- "Banks will charge me fees I do not know about" — this is a real risk, but reading the fee schedule and asking questions prevents it

**Handout:** Fee schedule comparison worksheet — help members compare two or three accounts using a simple grid
