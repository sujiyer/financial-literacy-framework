# Institution Adoption Guide

This guide walks a community bank, credit union, or CDFI through implementing the Financial Literacy Framework for their member population. It assumes limited staff time and no dedicated financial education budget.

---

## Step 1: Assess Your Member Population (Week 1)

Before choosing which modules to deploy first, understand who you are serving.

**Questions to answer:**
- What is the primary reason members come to us? (First account, loan, rebuilding credit?)
- What financial literacy gaps do frontline staff see most often?
- Do we serve a significant population of recent immigrants, first-generation bankers, or young adults?
- What languages does our membership primarily speak?

Use the Financial Literacy Gap Analyzer tool (tools/literacy-gap-analyzer.py) with FINRA data for your state to see objective gap data for your region.

---

## Step 2: Choose Your Starting Module (Week 1)

Do not try to implement all five modules at once. Start with one.

**Recommended first module by member profile:**

| Primary Member Need | Start With |
|---|---|
| Opening first accounts | Banking Basics |
| Denied credit, wants to rebuild | Credit and Loans |
| Ready to save for the future | Investing Basics |
| Confused by AI-assisted decisions | AI in Finance |
| Disputing a decision or filing a complaint | Understanding Your Rights |

---

## Step 3: Adapt the Content (Week 2)

The modules are starting points, not scripts. Adapt them for your specific context.

**Adaptation checklist:**
- [ ] Replace generic examples with examples relevant to your community
- [ ] Add your institution's specific products and fee structure where relevant
- [ ] Translate into the primary languages of your membership
- [ ] Add local resources — food banks, legal aid, financial counselling services specific to your area
- [ ] Review with your compliance officer to confirm accuracy for your regulatory context

---

## Step 4: Choose Your Delivery Format (Week 2)

The modules work in multiple formats. Choose what fits your capacity:

**In-person workshop:** 60 to 90 minutes. Best for building trust and answering questions. Requires a staff facilitator and a meeting space.

**One-on-one at account opening:** Integrate key points from the relevant module into your onboarding conversation. Takes 10 to 15 additional minutes per member.

**Printed handout:** Distill the module glossary and key points into a one-page handout given at account opening or with loan documents.

**Digital content:** Post adapted module content on your website, member portal, or social media as a series of short educational posts.

---

## Step 5: Run the Plain Language Converter on Your Documents (Week 3)

Use the plain language converter tool (tools/plain-language-converter.py) to audit your existing member communications.

**Documents to audit:**
- Account opening disclosure
- Fee schedule
- Loan agreement summary
- Account alert notifications
- Denial letters and adverse action notices

For each flagged term, decide: can we replace this term with plain language in the document itself? Or should we provide a glossary alongside the document?

---

## Step 6: Measure and Improve (Ongoing)

Track at least two metrics after implementing each module:

**Member comprehension:** Ask three or four simple questions at the end of a workshop or at the end of an onboarding session. What percentage of members can answer correctly?

**Support ticket reduction:** Does the volume of calls or visits about a specific topic go down after you have educated members about it? A reduction in "why was I charged this fee" calls after implementing the Banking Basics module is a measurable outcome.

---

## Low-Resource Implementation

If your institution has very limited staff capacity, start here:

**Minimum viable implementation — one hour of staff time per month:**
1. Print and distribute the Banking Basics glossary at every account opening
2. Run the plain language converter on your denial letters and simplify the language
3. Add the CFPB complaint URL to your denial letters: consumerfinance.gov/complaint

These three steps require almost no ongoing time investment and immediately improve the experience for members who are new to banking or who have experienced an adverse decision.
